## Overview
Freddy's behavioral profile is a self-assessment (no formal DISC/PI/MBTI assessment on file), synthesized from how he describes his own working style. He reads as an **autonomous, evidence-gated executor** - someone who wants full ownership of a technical scope, decides fast but only once a decision is grounded in verified signal, and moves through work in sequential, self-contained, reviewed steps rather than parallel churn.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive | Level | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Autonomy / Ownership | High | Comfortable owning a full technical scope end to end, solo or embedded in a small team, with clear ownership boundaries |
| Decision pace | Fast, but evidence-gated | Moves quickly, but wants the decision grounded in verified signal rather than speed for its own sake |
| Process structure | Sequential, self-contained | Works in discrete steps, each reviewed before the next begins, rather than many things in flight at once |
| Adaptability | High | Changes course quickly and without friction once new information shows the plan needs to shift |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **End-to-end technical ownership:** runs personal projects solo to 480+ commits with hexagonal/DDD architecture, and takes the same ownership posture inside a delivery team (e.g. designing and shipping the Media Value terms-and-conditions feature after senior sign-off)
- **Direct, practical communication:** favors concrete checklists and clear next steps over long comparative discussion
- **Low-friction course correction:** revises the plan quickly once new information arrives, without getting attached to the original approach

## How You Work Best
- Small, senior/competent teams with clear ownership boundaries
- Low-noise, structured environments that allow moving from planning straight to execution with few hand-offs
- Sequential, self-contained steps, each reviewed before the next begins

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Multi-stakeholder consensus cultures:** what drains Freddy isn't collaboration itself, it's the same decision being re-reviewed by many stakeholders before work can move. Frame this as a strength in front-loading context: writing proposals thorough enough that they need fewer review cycles, not as discomfort with feedback.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "ownership", "own the full stack / feature end to end"
- "small team", "senior team", "high-trust, low-oversight"
- "move fast", "autonomous", "async-first"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "consensus-driven culture", "committee sign-off", "heavy stakeholder alignment"
- "matrixed organization", "extensive approval chains"
- Frequent status meetings as the primary coordination mechanism

## Management Style Preferences
- Works well with: light-touch management that sets clear scope and steps back; async updates over frequent status meetings
- Doesn't work well with: the same decision needing repeated re-review by multiple stakeholders before work can proceed

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** reference end-to-end ownership of specific features (design through delivery) rather than generic "team player" language
- **CV:** keep the "end to end" framing already present in the experience bullets - domain, API, database, and interface in the same line
- **Interviews:** use the Media Value T&Cs feature (owned end to end after senior approval) and the TCI Factory-pattern integration (fast, evidence-based redesign once a second warehouse changed the requirements) as STAR examples for ownership and adaptability
- **Don't overstate:** no formal people-management or team-lead experience - the ownership described is individual-contributor scope, not managing others
