# Upwork Proposal Rules

## Structure — exactly three paragraphs, in this order

1. **You read the project.** One or two specific details pulled from *this* posting — not a
   generic "I read your job post." If you can't point to something specific, you haven't
   understood it well enough to write paragraph 2 yet.
2. **How you'd solve it, with your actual stack.** Concrete approach, not a skills list. Every
   technical claim must trace back to `01-candidate-profile.md` - no fabricated project
   experience, same grounding rule as `/apply-json`. If the project needs something adjacent to
   what's on the profile, say so honestly rather than implying direct experience.
3. **CTA.** Availability, and a specific next step (a question about scope, an offer to hop on a
   call) - something that invites a reply, not just "let me know if interested."

## Length

**5,000 characters is Upwork's hard ceiling, not the target.** Aim for 150-300 words
(roughly 900-1,800 characters) - clients see only the opening lines before expanding, and
proposals that read as padded to fill space perform worse, not better. Shorter is fine for a
simple project; use the extra room only when the project genuinely needs more explanation.

## Language

Always English, regardless of the conversation language (Spanish) or the posting's own
language. Upwork is an English-first platform.

## Tone

Direct and specific, per `03-writing-style.md` and the behavioral profile in
`02-candidate-profile.md`. No generic template language ("I am a passionate developer with
experience in..."). A proposal that could be sent unchanged to 20 other jobs is not
specific enough - rewrite it.

## Selection filter (what's worth drafting for)

Before drafting, sanity-check the project against these (Freddy's own stated targeting
criteria) - if it clearly fails, say so and ask before spending effort on a full draft:

- Fewer than ~10 existing proposals (posted recently, not buried)
- Fixed-price preferred over hourly, unless hourly is clearly a good fit
- Genuine match to React/Next.js/TypeScript/Node.js - not a stretch
- Budget isn't a clear underquote for the scope described (see the anti-underquoting note in
  `02-local-prospects.md` - the same math applies here)

## Never

Never submit the proposal. This produces text only - Freddy copies and pastes it into Upwork
himself.
